# MediFlow User Guide

## Administrator

1. Open **Departments** and create clinical departments.
2. Open **Doctors** to create doctor accounts and clinical profiles.
3. Open **Schedules** to assign recurring hours and leave exceptions.
4. Open **Clinical Catalogs** to configure lab tests, medicines, allergies and medicine safety tags.
5. Use **Users & Roles** for reception, laboratory and additional admin accounts.
6. Review dashboards, reports and audit logs.

## Receptionist

1. Register walk-in or emergency patients from **Patients**.
2. Book appointments from **Appointments**.
3. Confirm pending patient portal bookings.
4. Open **Patient Queue** and check in arriving patients.
5. Open **Billing** to record discounts, charges and payments.

## Doctor

1. Review **Dashboard** or **Patient Queue**.
2. Start a checked-in appointment.
3. Record complaints, symptoms, vital signs, diagnosis and notes.
4. Add medicines. MediFlow checks medicine allergy tags against patient allergies.
5. Provide an override reason when clinically continuing despite a warning.
6. Request laboratory tests and set a follow-up date.
7. Save a draft or complete the consultation.
8. Print prescriptions from **Prescriptions**.

## Laboratory operator

1. Open **Laboratory**.
2. Update a test from Requested → Sample Collected → Processing → Completed.
3. Enter a result value or summary.
4. Upload a PDF/JPG/PNG report when available.
5. The patient receives an internal notification after completion.

## Patient

1. Register from the login page or use a receptionist-created record.
2. Book appointments by department, doctor, date and available slot.
3. View appointment status and queue information.
4. Open prescriptions, laboratory reports and invoices.
5. Review follow-up recommendations and notifications.

## Demonstration workflow

For a project presentation:

1. Admin creates a department, doctor and schedule.
2. Receptionist registers a patient with a Penicillin allergy.
3. Patient/receptionist books the doctor.
4. Receptionist checks in the patient and receives a token.
5. Doctor starts the consultation and selects Amoxicillin.
6. The allergy warning appears; doctor changes medicine or records an override reason.
7. Doctor requests CBC and completes the consultation.
8. Lab operator completes CBC and uploads a report.
9. Receptionist records payment.
10. Patient views the prescription, report and invoice.
11. Admin opens reports and audit logs.
